Ash Handling Plant Working Principle Explained (Bottom Ash + Fly Ash)

By a working Assistant Engineer, 500 MW Thermal Power Station · Updated August 2026 · 9 min read

Every kilogram of coal burnt in a thermal power plant leaves behind ash — roughly 30-40% of Indian coal (by weight) turns into ash after combustion. Handling this ash safely and continuously is the job of the Ash Handling Plant (AHP), and it's one of the most commonly asked topics in AE/JE mechanical interviews. I run this system daily, so let's break it down the way it actually works on-site.

Two Types of Ash

Combustion in a pulverised-fuel boiler produces two distinct ash streams:

  • Bottom Ash (~20% of total ash): Heavier ash particles that fall to the bottom of the furnace hopper.
  • Fly Ash (~80% of total ash): Lighter, fine ash particles that travel with the flue gas and are captured downstream, mainly in the Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP).

Bottom Ash Handling System

Bottom ash falls from the furnace into a water-filled bottom ash hopper, which quenches (cools) the hot ash and forms a seal that prevents furnace gases from escaping. From here, one of these methods removes the ash:

  • Jet pumping / hydro-ejector system: High-pressure water jets flush the ash-water slurry through pipelines to the ash slurry sump.
  • Submerged Scraper Conveyor (SSC): A drag-chain conveyor submerged in water continuously drags bottom ash out of the hopper onto a conveyor belt — increasingly common in modern plants because it uses far less water than jetting systems.

From the sump, ash slurry is pumped to the ash disposal area or ash pond using ash slurry pumps, often in series (booster pumps) over long distances.

Fly Ash Handling System

Fly ash is captured before flue gas exits through the chimney, mainly by the Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP), which electrically charges ash particles and collects them on grounded collection plates. Ash is knocked off the plates by rapping mechanisms and falls into hoppers below each ESP field.

From the ESP hoppers, fly ash is conveyed using one of these systems:

  • Vacuum system: Ash is sucked through pipelines using vacuum pumps/exhausters to a central storage silo — common where ash needs to be kept dry for sale to cement industries.
  • Pressure conveying system: Compressed air pushes fly ash through pipelines from hoppers to the storage silo.

From the silo, dry fly ash is either dispatched to cement/brick manufacturers (dry ash utilisation) or mixed with water to form slurry and pumped to the ash pond, depending on the plant's ash utilisation policy.

Why Ash Handling Matters for Plant Reliability

A choked bottom ash hopper or a stuck SSC can force a boiler load reduction or even a unit trip, since ash has nowhere to go. Similarly, if ESP hoppers aren't evacuated in time, ash can build up and damage precipitator internals. This is why AHP is monitored around the clock in the control room, with hopper level indicators and conveyor running status as key parameters.

Interview Questions & Answers

Q1. What percentage of total ash is typically bottom ash vs fly ash in a pulverised coal boiler?

A: Roughly 20% bottom ash and 80% fly ash, though this varies with coal quality and boiler design.

Q2. What is the function of the bottom ash hopper water seal?

A: It quenches hot ash falling from the furnace and forms a water seal that prevents furnace gases and pressure from escaping through the bottom of the furnace.

Q3. What is a Submerged Scraper Conveyor (SSC) and why is it preferred in modern plants?

A: An SSC is a drag-chain conveyor submerged in the bottom ash hopper's water bath that continuously drags out bottom ash. It's preferred over jetting systems because it uses significantly less water and produces less ash slurry to dispose of.

Q4. How is fly ash captured before the flue gas leaves the chimney?

A: By the Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP), which charges ash particles electrically and collects them on grounded plates; collected ash is dislodged by rapping and falls into hoppers below.

Q5. What are the two main methods of conveying dry fly ash from ESP hoppers to the storage silo?

A: Vacuum conveying (suction through pipelines) and pressure conveying (compressed air pushing ash through pipelines).

Quick Revision Points

  • Ash splits roughly 20% bottom ash, 80% fly ash.
  • Bottom ash hopper: water-quenched, water-sealed against furnace pressure.
  • Bottom ash removal: jetting/hydro-ejector system or Submerged Scraper Conveyor (SSC).
  • Fly ash captured by ESP, removed via vacuum or pressure conveying systems.
  • Ash finally goes to ash pond (slurry) or is sold dry for cement/brick manufacturing.
