MPPGCL AE Mechanical Exam — Syllabus, Pattern & Preparation Strategy

By a working Assistant Engineer, 500 MW Thermal Power Station · Updated August 2026 · 10 min read

Madhya Pradesh Power Generating Company Limited (MPPGCL) regularly recruits Assistant Engineers (AE) in the Mechanical discipline for its thermal, hydro and other generating stations. Having gone through this exact recruitment ecosystem myself, here's an honest, exam-focused breakdown of the syllabus, pattern and a preparation strategy that actually works.

MPPGCL AE Mechanical — Exam Pattern Overview

StageModeApprox. DurationNature
Online Written ExamComputer-based (objective/MCQ)~2-3 hoursTechnical + General Aptitude
Document VerificationIn-person—Certificates & eligibility check
Interview (if applicable)In-person—Technical + personality assessment

Note: Exact pattern, marking scheme and number of stages can change with each recruitment cycle — always cross-check the latest official notification on the MPPGCL website before applying.

Syllabus Breakdown — Mechanical Engineering Core

  • Thermodynamics: Laws of thermodynamics, Rankine cycle, Brayton cycle, entropy, availability
  • Power Plant Engineering: Boiler types, draught systems, ash handling, coal handling, condensers, cooling towers, feed water systems
  • Steam Turbines & Boilers: Impulse vs reaction turbines, governing systems, boiler mountings and accessories
  • Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulic Machines: Pumps, turbines, flow measurement
  • Heat Transfer: Conduction, convection, radiation, heat exchangers
  • Strength of Materials: Stress-strain, bending, torsion, columns
  • Theory of Machines & Machine Design: Gears, cams, balancing, design of shafts and fasteners
  • Industrial Engineering & Production: Basic manufacturing processes, quality control
  • General Aptitude & Reasoning: Quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, English
  • General Awareness: Current affairs, MP state GK, power sector awareness

Why Power Plant Engineering Deserves Extra Attention

Since MPPGCL is a power generation company, questions on boiler systems, draught systems, ash handling, coal handling, condensers and cooling towers are disproportionately common compared to a generic GATE-style mechanical exam. This is exactly the gap most candidates from a pure academic background struggle with — because these topics are barely covered in college and are best learned from someone who actually operates them.

Week-by-Week Preparation Strategy

  1. Weeks 1-2: Build/revise core fundamentals — thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, SOM. Use standard GATE-level mechanical engineering references.
  2. Weeks 3-4: Deep-dive into power plant engineering specifically — boiler systems, draught, ash handling, coal handling, turbines, condensers. This is where plant-specific resources (like our articles and Field Guide eBook) save the most time.
  3. Weeks 5-6: Practice topic-wise MCQs daily; identify weak areas and revise them immediately, don't leave for later.
  4. Week 7: Take full-length mock tests under timed conditions; analyse mistakes rather than just scores.
  5. Week 8: Focus on general awareness, MP state GK and quick revision notes; avoid starting new topics this close to the exam.

Interview Questions & Answers

Q1. What subjects carry the most weight in the MPPGCL AE Mechanical technical section?

A: Power plant engineering (boiler, turbine, ash/coal handling systems) typically carries significant weight alongside core mechanical subjects like thermodynamics, fluid mechanics and strength of materials, since MPPGCL is a power generation utility.

Q2. Is prior power plant experience necessary to clear the MPPGCL AE exam?

A: No, but candidates without hands-on exposure should specifically study power plant-focused material (like plant system articles and field guides) since these topics aren't covered in depth in most engineering curricula.

Q3. How should I split my time between core mechanical subjects and power plant-specific topics?

A: A practical split is roughly 60% on core mechanical fundamentals (thermodynamics, SOM, fluid mechanics, TOM) and 40% specifically on power plant systems, since the latter is where most candidates lose easy marks.

Quick Revision Points

  • MPPGCL AE Mechanical exam = objective written test, often followed by document verification/interview.
  • Core mechanical subjects + power plant engineering both carry significant weight.
  • Power plant systems (boiler, draught, ash/coal handling) are the differentiator — most candidates under-prepare here.
  • Always verify the latest exam pattern from the official MPPGCL notification before your attempt.
