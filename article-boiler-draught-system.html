Boiler Draught System — Interview Questions & Answers for AE/JE Exams

By a working Assistant Engineer, 500 MW Thermal Power Station · Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

If there's one topic that shows up in almost every AE/JE mechanical interview and written exam, it's the boiler draught system. It sounds simple on paper, but examiners love it because it tests whether you actually understand airflow through a boiler — not just memorised definitions. Having worked on draught systems daily at a 500 MW thermal station, here's the explanation I wish I'd had during my own exam prep.

What is Draught in a Boiler?

Draught is the pressure difference that causes air to flow into the furnace for combustion and flue gas to flow out through the chimney/stack. Without draught, coal cannot burn efficiently and flue gases cannot be expelled. Draught can be created naturally (by a chimney) or mechanically (by fans).

Types of Draught Systems

1. Natural Draught

Created purely by the height of the chimney and the density difference between hot flue gas inside the chimney and cooler ambient air outside. No fans are involved. It's simple but weak — mostly seen in small, old boilers, not in modern high-capacity units.

2. Forced Draught (FD)

An FD fan pushes fresh air into the furnace from the intake side, creating a positive pressure inside the furnace. This is the "push" side of the system — it forces air through the air preheater (APH) and into the burners for combustion.

3. Induced Draught (ID)

An ID fan is placed after the furnace, near the chimney, and "pulls" flue gases out of the furnace, through the economiser and air preheater, and finally out the stack. This creates a slight negative pressure inside the furnace.

4. Balanced Draught

Most modern high-capacity boilers (like the ones used in NTPC and state genco stations) use balanced draught — a combination of FD and ID fans working together. The FD fan pushes air in, the ID fan pulls flue gas out, and together they maintain the furnace at a slightly negative pressure (typically -2 to -5 mm WC). This prevents flue gas and flame from blowing out through furnace openings while still ensuring efficient combustion and gas removal.

Why Furnace Pressure Control Matters

On-site, furnace draught pressure is monitored continuously by the control room. If it swings too positive, hot flue gas and flame can blow back through furnace inspection doors and burner openings — a serious safety hazard. If it swings too negative, excess cold air gets pulled in through leaks, reducing boiler efficiency. This is exactly why balanced draught with automatic control (via FD/ID fan vane or speed control) is standard in modern plants.

Interview Questions & Answers

Q1. What is the difference between forced draught and induced draught?

A: Forced draught uses a fan to push air into the furnace (positive pressure, before combustion), while induced draught uses a fan to pull flue gas out of the furnace towards the chimney (negative pressure, after combustion).

Q2. Why do modern boilers use balanced draught instead of only FD or only ID?

A: Balanced draught maintains a slightly negative furnace pressure using both FD and ID fans together. This avoids flame/gas blow-out (which happens with pure forced draught) and avoids excess air ingress through leaks (which happens with pure induced draught), giving better safety and combustion efficiency.

Q3. What is the typical furnace draught pressure maintained in a balanced draught boiler?

A: Typically around -2 to -5 mm of water column (WC), monitored and controlled continuously from the control room using FD/ID fan vane position or variable speed drives.

Q4. What happens if the ID fan trips while the FD fan is still running?

A: Furnace pressure rises rapidly (goes positive) since flue gas is no longer being pulled out while air is still being pushed in. This can cause flame and hot gases to blow back through furnace openings — most boiler protection logics will trip the unit (MFT — Master Fuel Trip) to prevent damage and ensure safety.

Q5. Where are the FD and ID fans located relative to the boiler?

A: The FD fan is located before the air preheater and furnace, on the air intake side. The ID fan is located after the economiser and air preheater, near the chimney, on the flue gas exit side.

Quick Revision Points

  • Draught = pressure difference driving air in / flue gas out of the furnace.
  • Natural draught: chimney effect only, no fans.
  • Forced draught: FD fan, positive furnace pressure.
  • Induced draught: ID fan, negative furnace pressure.
  • Balanced draught: FD + ID together, slightly negative furnace pressure — standard in modern plants.
  • ID fan trip → furnace pressure rises → risk of flame blow-out → protection trips the unit.
